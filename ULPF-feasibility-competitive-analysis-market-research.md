# Universal Log Pre-processing Framework (ULPF / SIH26156): Deep-Dive Feasibility, Competitive & Market Report

## TL;DR
- **ULPF is technically feasible and strategically well-timed, but it is not a greenfield: every major commercial pipeline vendor (Cribl, Splunk Edge Processor, Datadog, Microsoft Sentinel) already ships OCSF or OCSF-adjacent normalization, so ULPF's defensible edge is not "OCSF conversion" per se but sovereign, air-gapped, open-source, lossless normalization of Indian perimeter-device logs tuned to CERT-In/NCIIPC/SEBI mandates — a niche none of the incumbents serve well.**
- **The regulatory tailwind is real and quantifiable:** CERT-In's 180-day log-retention mandate, SEBI's CSCRF, RBI's IT framework, and NCIIPC's CII guidelines all force Indian entities to collect, retain and standardize logs; Gartner projects India information-security spending at $3.4B in 2026 (+11.7%), with security software the largest and fastest-growing segment at $1.56B (+12.4%) and SIEM explicitly named as a driver.
- **A viable "path beyond SIH" exists but is not guaranteed:** the strongest funded pathways are MeitY–DSCI's Cyber Security Grand Challenge (₹6.85 crore pool), iDEX/DISC cyber challenges (grants up to ₹1.5–10 crore), and NCIIPC's own indigenous-developer proposal channel — but documented SIH-to-procurement success stories are thin, so the team should treat procurement as something to be engineered, not assumed.

## Key Findings

1. **OCSF normalization is already commoditized at the top of the market.** Cribl Stream has shipped "full support for OCSF" since November 2022 (Amazon Security Lake launch partner), Splunk Edge Processor added native `ocsf`/`to_ocsf` SPL2 commands (GA in Splunk Cloud 9.2.2406+), and Datadog Cloud SIEM bases its default data model on OCSF and offers a self-service OCSF processor for any log source. Microsoft Sentinel still uses its own ASIM (not OCSF), with OCSF only "on the roadmap." ULPF cannot win on "we convert to OCSF" alone.

2. **Licensing and deployment model is the real differentiation axis.** The Elastic SSPL relicensing saga (Apache 2.0 → SSPL/ELv2 in 2021, AGPLv3 added 2024) is a cautionary tale for anyone building on Elastic. For a government/CII tool, a permissive OSS core (Apache 2.0 / MIT) plus true air-gapped deployment is a hard requirement that rules out cloud-managed control planes (Cribl.Cloud, Splunk Edge Processor's cloud management, Microsoft Sentinel, Google SecOps, Datadog). NXLog, Vector, Fluent Bit and syslog-ng are the realistic building blocks.

3. **Mature, permissively-licensed building blocks exist** — the team should assemble, not reinvent. The OCSF project publishes an official schema repo and an `ocsf/examples` mapping repo; Apache Camel ships an OCSF data format (34 event classes, 68 objects); open CEF parsers exist; and OCSF v1.x defines the exact event classes ULPF needs for perimeter devices (Network Activity category_uid 4 → Network Activity 4001, HTTP Activity 4002, plus Web Application Firewall 6005).

4. **The "universal/lossless" claim is the biggest technical risk.** Real-world OCSF adopters report 2–4 month mapping projects, an "unmapped field" problem, schema-drift failures (a single integer→string field change silently killing an EDR pipeline), and query-time-vs-ingest-time normalization tradeoffs. A technical judge will probe exactly these. ULPF must treat lossless-ness (raw log preservation alongside normalized output) and drift detection as first-class features.

5. **Market sizing from primary sources confirms the opportunity but at India-specific scale.** Gartner (primary) puts India infosec end-user spending at $3.4B in 2026; Cisco's FY2025 10-K states security revenue increased "by 59%, or $3.0 billion, primarily driven by Threat Intelligence, Detection, and Response offerings, which includes the offerings from Splunk" (to $8.094B, up from $5.075B), with Splunk ARR ~$4.3B — evidence of how valuable the log/SIEM layer is. Indian-specific SIEM/log-management line-item figures remain unavailable from primary sources; secondary aggregators (MarketsandMarkets, whose report states "the log management & SIEM segment dominates" the India cybersecurity market of $8.58B in 2025→$16.86B by 2030 at 14.5% CAGR; Mordor $6.56B for 2026) remain the best available proxies.

### 3. Regulatory deep dive

- **CERT-In Directions (28 April 2022, in force 27 June 2022; MSME extension to 25 Sept 2022):** issued under §70B(6) IT Act 2000. Core mandates: enable and **maintain ICT-system logs for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction**; report specified cyber incidents **within 6 hours**; NTP sync to NPL/NIC; VASPs/VPN/data centres KYC + 5-year financial-record retention. Non-compliance punishable with up to 1 year imprisonment and/or ₹1 lakh fine. FAQs (18 May 2022, 44 items) clarified logs may sit abroad if a copy is kept in India, and individuals are out of scope.
- **Criticism (feeds the risk section):** the Internet Society's Internet Impact Brief called the 180-day-all-logs + government-time-sync mandate "overbroad," warning of a "one size fits all" repository burden especially on SMEs; Cyril Amarchand and others flagged the undefined breadth of "ICT systems."
- **Sector overlays:** **SEBI CSCRF** (effective ~August 2024) mandates centralized log collection, NTP sync, tamper-proof storage, and audit-log retention (commonly cited 1–2 years operationally, up to 5 years for audit logs per SEBI-registered entity obligations), plus 6-hour incident reporting aligned to CERT-In, with a 180-day/in-India baseline "baked in." **RBI** IT-governance Master Direction (effective April 2024) and SAR/CSITE guidance for banks/NBFCs. **IRDAI** cyber guidelines for insurers. **NCIIPC** (a unit of NTRO under §70A) issues CII guidelines organized on Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover, explicitly expecting "SIEM with CII-specific use cases" and 24×7 monitoring across eight critical sectors (Power & Energy; BFSI; Telecom; Transport; Government; Strategic & Public Enterprises; plus health and others). NCIIPC operates under the Information Security Practices and Procedures for Protected System Rules, 2018.
- **International parallels (precedent/driver for OCSF):** **EU NIS2** (mandatory Oct 2024) requires logging/audit trails (commonly cited ~12-month retention) and 24-hour incident reporting for essential/important entities across energy, transport, finance, health, digital infrastructure; **DORA** (in force Jan 2025) adds ICT-risk logging, full logging coverage, and 72h/1-month incident reporting for financial entities. These regimes are accelerating OCSF/log-standardization adoption globally and are useful precedent for ULPF's framing.

### 4. OCSF technical deep dive

- **Taxonomy:** OCSF organizes telemetry into **Categories → Event Classes → (Profiles, Extensions)**, with each event carrying core metadata (time, severity_id, class_uid, category_uid, activity_id, metadata). `type_uid = (class_uid × 100) + activity_id`. Current schema is v1.x (v1.1/1.2 documented by AWS; Cribl referencing v1.6; project ~v1.9 per prior research). Categories include System Activity (1), Findings (2), IAM (3), **Network Activity (4)**, Discovery (5), Application Activity (6), Remediation (7).
- **Perimeter-device-relevant classes:** Network Activity **4001**, HTTP Activity **4002**, plus DNS/DHCP/RDP/SMB/SSH/FTP Activity; **Web Application Firewall Activity (6005)** under Application. A firewall log (`src_ip/dst_ip/dst_port/action/proto`) maps to Network Activity 4001; NGFW/IDS/WAF HTTP traffic maps to HTTP Activity 4002. This is exactly ULPF's target surface.
- **Known limitations/criticisms:** (a) **schema complexity/integration overhead** — hierarchical taxonomy + strict naming makes real mappings a "sequence of judgment calls"; (b) **the "unmapped field" problem** — richer sources (e.g., Zeek's connection uid, directory-tagged identifiers in ASIM/ECS) can *lose* provenance when flattened into OCSF (Security Data Works: mapping into a "richer" schema can still lose information); (c) **no MITRE ATT&CK linkage guidance** (Solutions Review); (d) **uneven adoption / vendor resistance** — some vendors would rather be the aggregator others map to; (e) OCSF standardizes *format*, not storage — "there's no magic super data lake."
- **Competing schemas:** **ECS (Elastic Common Schema)** — the main rival, flat namespace, observability-oriented; **ASIM** (Microsoft, aligned to OSSEM); **UDM** (Google, proprietary, needs a bridge such as Synqly Mesh to reach OCSF); **STIX/TAXII** (threat-intel exchange, different problem); **CIM** (Splunk). OCSF has the strongest cross-vendor consortium momentum (Linux Foundation, AWS, Splunk, Cisco, IBM, CrowdStrike, SentinelOne) but ECS is more entrenched in existing deployments.
- **Reusable open-source starting points (build-on, don't rebuild):** `ocsf/ocsf-schema` (core schema), `ocsf/examples` (raw→OCSF mappings by DSL), **Apache Camel OCSF data format** (34 classes/68 objects, JSON marshal/unmarshal), open CEF parsers (jcustenborder/cef-parser, kamushadenes/cefevent), Sigma/pySigma + OSSEM for field translation, and Splunk's free OCSF-CIM add-on. A hackathon team can wire Vector/Fluent Bit (ingest) → mapping layer (Camel/ocsf examples) → OCSF JSON, with raw retained.

### 6. Risk / critique deep dive

- **CERT-In 180-day mandate critiques:** overbroad "all ICT logs" scope (Internet Society IIAT), disproportionate SME burden, undefined "ICT systems," privacy/data-minimization tension (contrast with DPDP Act 2023's minimization ethos and NIS2/DORA guidance to minimize and pseudonymize log data). ULPF should support configurable retention, field-level redaction/pseudonymization, and tamper-evident storage to sit on the right side of both mandates.
- **"Universal normalization" technical failure modes a judge will raise:**
  - **Lossy transformation:** flattening rich sources into OCSF drops provenance (the "unmapped field" bucket); mitigation = always retain raw log + observables array + unmapped map.
  - **Schema drift:** a silent vendor field-type change (integer→string) can halt a parser and cause silent data loss (Databahn's documented 2:47 AM EDR blackout scenario); mitigation = in-flight drift detection, schema validation, dead-letter queues.
  - **Performance overhead at scale:** real-time normalization is CPU-bound; Fluentd is GIL-limited, Fluent Bit plateaus ~31.3k logs/s (VictoriaMetrics 2026), query-time parsing (ASIM) slows large queries. ULPF must publish throughput benchmarks and choose a Rust/C data path (Vector/Fluent Bit class, ~86 MiB/s achievable) for credibility.
  - **Mapping is judgment-heavy:** 2–4 month enterprise OCSF projects are typical even for well-resourced teams; a hackathon MVP should scope to a *handful* of perimeter-device source types done losslessly rather than "any hardware or software."
  - **No storage/analytics in OCSF itself:** ULPF still needs a backend (Parseable, OpenSearch, ClickHouse); Parseable (Bengaluru OSS) is a natural sovereign pairing.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Hackathon MVP scope (now):** Build ingest (Vector or Fluent Bit, both OSS + air-gapped) → a mapping layer reusing `ocsf/examples` + Apache Camel OCSF format → OCSF v1.x JSON output, for **3–5 concrete perimeter-device source types** (e.g., one firewall in Syslog/CEF, one router, one IDS/IPS), each mapped **losslessly** (raw preserved, observables array, explicit `unmapped` bucket). Ship a schema-drift detector and per-source throughput numbers. This directly answers the "lossy/drift/perf" critiques judges will raise. **Benchmark to hit:** demonstrate ≥50k events/s single-node and <5% field loss on a labeled test set.

**Stage 2 — Differentiation hardening (post-SIH, 0–6 months):** Lock in the three-part moat incumbents lack — **permissive OSS license (Apache 2.0), verified air-gapped/containerized deployment, and Indian-regulation presets** (CERT-In 180-day retention, SEBI audit-log retention, NTP-to-NPL, field-level pseudonymization for DPDP). Pair with Parseable/OpenSearch for a fully sovereign stack. Deliverable: a reproducible air-gapped install + a CERT-In/SEBI/NCIIPC compliance mapping doc.

**Stage 3 — Funded pathway (3–12 months), pursue in parallel:**
1. Submit the product proposal to **NCIIPC (helpdesk2@nciipc.gov.in)** — most direct given NTRO/NCIIPC sponsorship.
2. Enter the next **MeitY–DSCI Cyber Security Grand Challenge** (staged funding to ₹1 crore, IPR retained).
3. If a defence-services use case can be framed, target **iDEX DISC cyber** (₹1.5–10 crore, explicit procurement path).
4. Use **TIDE 2.0 / a MeitY Startup Hub incubator** for early prototype grants (₹4–7 lakh) to bridge to the above.

**Benchmarks that would change the plan:** If Microsoft Sentinel ships native OCSF (currently roadmap) or if an Indian OSS competitor (e.g., a Parseable-adjacent normalizer) launches OCSF-native air-gapped support, the differentiation narrows — pivot to depth of Indian-device coverage and compliance automation. If CERT-In/SEBI issue a specific *log-format* standard (they currently mandate retention, not format), align ULPF's output to it immediately, as that would create a mandated market.

## Caveats
- **Estimates vs primary data:** India-specific SIEM/log-management market size is **not available from primary sources**; MarketsandMarkets/Mordor figures are secondary estimates and vary widely (India cybersecurity 2025: $5.56B Mordor vs $8.58B M&M vs $10.16B Grand View) — cite as ranges, not facts. Gartner's $3.4B India infosec 2026 and Cisco/Datadog filings are the reliable primary anchors.
- **SIH-to-procurement precedent is weakly documented:** the official framework is real, but verified named deployment case studies could not be found; do not overstate the pipeline.
- **OCSF version drift:** sources cite v1.1–v1.6 depending on date; confirm the exact current version (~1.9 per prior research) before building.
- **Vendor claims:** throughput numbers (86 MiB/s Vector, 31.3k logs/s Fluent Bit, "1.2 TB/hr Vector") are vendor/blog benchmarks on differing hardware and versions — treat as directional, not comparable head-to-head.
- **NIS2/DORA/SEBI retention figures** vary by source and entity tier; the specific numbers (≈12 months NIS2, up to 5 years SEBI audit logs, 180 days CERT-In) should be re-verified against the primary legal text for any compliance-critical use.
- Some competitor cells (Mezmo OCSF support, exact syslog-ng OCSF capability) are inferred from general documentation rather than a definitive vendor statement and are flagged accordingly.
